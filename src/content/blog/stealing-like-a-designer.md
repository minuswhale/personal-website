I spent a week clicking through the personal-blog shelf of Awwwards. Not to copy — to *steal*, in the Austin Kleon sense: to figure out why the winners win, then take only the principles.

## One accent color, three places

Every site that felt cohesive used a single accent color, and used it sparingly. One site used burnt orange for exactly three things: an italic word in the headline, the active nav state, and list bullets. Another used coral red *only for periods* — the wordmark, the headline, the end of sentences. Restraint reads as confidence.

## The type trio

The strongest sites paired three typefaces, each with exactly one job:

- a **serif display** for headlines (editorial, human)
- a **sans** for body copy (readable, quiet)
- a **mono** for metadata — dates, tags, eyebrows, index numbers (engineer energy)

That last one surprised me. Mono metadata is the cheapest trick in the set and the most effective: `003 · Nov 2026 · 6 min read` instantly makes a blog feel like a *publication* rather than a feed.

## Blogs are not card grids

Almost nobody used cards. The best post lists were flat, editorial lists: generous whitespace, hairline or dotted separators, index numbers, years grouped in a margin column. A card grid says "dashboard". A flat list says "author's desk".

## One signature element

Each winning site had exactly one memorable move — a draggable chrome-lettered name, a golden thread woven through contour lines, a terminal ticker scrolling code beneath the hero. One idea, executed everywhere, beats five ideas executed halfway.

## What I took

For this site: warm charcoal instead of pure black, one orange accent, the serif/sans/mono trio, a publication-style post list, and a terminal ticker under the hero. The rest I left behind — deliberately.

> Good artists copy; great artists steal — and return the rest.
